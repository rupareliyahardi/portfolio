01. Rivayat

A contemporary residence inspired by the warmth and richness of Indian traditions. Rivayat combines earthy materials, handcrafted details, traditional patterns and modern forms to create an interior that feels both culturally rooted and suited to contemporary living.

A functional layout focusing on spatial zoning, circulation, and furniture placement for comfortable living.

The residence explores a contemporary expression of Indian heritage through material, craft, and detailing. Warm wood, cane, patterned surfaces, earthy tones, and handcrafted elements are carried throughout the living, dining, kitchen, and bedroom spaces to create a cohesive visual language. Traditional forms such as jali screens, carved wooden details, patterned tiles, and woven textures are reinterpreted within clean and functional spaces. Large openings bring in natural light and connect the interiors with the landscape, while the courtyard and outdoor areas extend the living experience beyond the built form. The overall design creates a home that feels warm, rooted, and timeless while remaining comfortable for contemporary living.

02. Kasuriya

Kasuriya is a lighting design project inspired by the rich craft heritage of Karnataka. Drawing from the geometric motifs and intricate stitch language of Kasuti embroidery, the lamp explores how traditional craft can be interpreted through a contemporary product.

The design brings together craft, material, and light to create a functional object that reflects cultural identity while fitting naturally into modern spaces.

process images.

Kasuriya is a lighting design project that explores the relationship between traditional Indian craftsmanship and contemporary product design. The project draws inspiration from Kasuti embroidery, a traditional craft from Karnataka known for its intricate geometric patterns, symbolic motifs, and detailed hand-stitched compositions.

The aim was to understand how the visual language of a traditional craft could be translated into a modern functional object without losing its cultural character. Instead of directly reproducing the embroidery, the design interprets its patterns, symmetry, repetition, and handcrafted quality through the form and detailing of a table lamp.

The lamp combines a muslin shade with an MDF base, creating a balance between softness and structure. The translucent quality of muslin allows light to diffuse gently, while the detailing inspired by Kasuti adds visual depth and connects the object to its craft origin.

The project focuses on creating a product where light becomes a medium for expressing craft and culture. Kasuriya represents an attempt to bridge the gap between heritage and contemporary living by transforming a traditional craft language into a simple, functional, and visually refined lighting piece.

03. Coffee Table

This coffee table explores the idea of modularity and multifunctionality through a stacking system. Each element is designed as a repeatable unit that can be rearranged into different forms and configurations.

The project focuses on the relationship between stone and wood, combining the solidity of Makrana marble and Kota stone with the warmth of a teak wood top. The design moves beyond a conventional coffee table by allowing its individual components to be adapted for other uses within a space.

Process

The design was developed by exploring the properties of stone and metal, followed by experimentation with form, arrangement, and material combinations.

The MDF table top was shaped and sanded to achieve smooth, curved edges. The teak wood components were carved using a lathe machine and divided into individual parts. The elements were then assembled as a stacking system to explore different permutations and combinations.

The original material concept included red Italian marble, Makrana marble, and yellow onyx, which was later reinterpreted using Makrana marble, Kota stone, and teak wood. The teak top was finished through putty, sanding, and colouring to achieve the final surface.

The final design is a modular coffee table made from a combination of Makrana marble, Kota stone, and a finished teak wood top.

Its individual elements can be stacked, rearranged, and reused, allowing the furniture to take on different configurations. The components can also function as partition elements or furniture legs, extending the product beyond its primary purpose as a coffee table.

The final outcome demonstrates how modularity, material exploration, and multifunctionality can be integrated into a single furniture piece, creating a design that can adapt to different spatial and functional requirements.
